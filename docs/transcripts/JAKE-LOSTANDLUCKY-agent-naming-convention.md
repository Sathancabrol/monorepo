## Spoken (verbatim, transcription IA du reel)

A company paid me $120 grand to teach their people that agents are just a naming convention, and the value comes from everything else. The instructions you write, what the AI can reach, and the outcome you actually want. I'm going to show you the whole setup right here, and you don't have to comment the word "agent" to get it.

Okay, this drives me nuts. In computing, everything good got built on top of something that already worked, and right now, everybody's out here building their own AI agent from scratch, like everybody. That's a brand new wheel, and the wheel is done.

An agent is really three parts. The instructions, so what you want done and how you'd do it. The model, which is the part you rent — Claude or ChatGPT or Copilot, I genuinely don't care which. And what it's allowed to reach, your files and your tools. The looping and the tool calls — a good harness, like Claude Code, already handles that. So for most people, the agent problem is solved. One good model, a good harness, and a good set of folders.

I watched this click in a room in Dubai. One person wrote a brief with Claude. Claude couldn't reach the government website it needed, so somebody on a completely different team ran the exact same brief in ChatGPT, and it worked. Same instructions in a different AI, and it still worked. The part worth owning was the instructions the whole time.

Now, if you've built an agent, I'd honestly love to see it, and an automation people can talk to is a great thing to have. The name just gets in the way of the part that matters. And Gartner says up to 70% of companies will walk away from the agent vendors built for them by 2028.

So write down the outcome you want, how you do it, what it's allowed to touch, and where a person checks it. Put that in a folder, point one good agent at it, and that one agent turns into every agent you need — which is exactly why…
